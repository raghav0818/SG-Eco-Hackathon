# Tray Watch video: research and field shoot plan

**Prepared 23 September 2026.** This is a plan for a video, not a report of results. At the time of writing, this checkout contains no field `frames.csv`, `readings.csv`, or `daily.csv`; every result in the finished video must come from the footage and notes collected at the stall.

## The video to make

Make **one 60–75 second, evidence-led field-test video** titled *“Tray Watch: what happened to one tray during service?”* Use the real cai png stall, one named vegetable tray, the mounted camera, its time-stamped images, and the owner's response if she agrees to be recorded. End with the actual observation and the next decision that observation could inform. Export a **silent 35–40 second loop** from the same edit for the 2 October showcase table, where someone may walk up halfway through. Keep the Pi/camera and the one-page report beside the screen.

This choice is tailored to the event. The organizer explicitly suggests a looping laptop video and says judges weigh problem understanding, originality, feasibility, potential impact, and pitch clarity equally. The scale-up presentation must distinguish the vision from what was built, say how, where, and with whom it was tested, and show actual findings and impact. Build and testing photos are requested; generic AI imagery cannot substitute for user interaction. See the [kickoff transcript](../hackathon%20documents/20260826%20SG%20Eco%20Loop%20Kickoff.transcript.vtt) (00:12–00:14, 02:27–02:31) and the project's [existing showcase concept](05-traywatch-plan.md) (§ “What goes on the 1.2 m table on 2 Oct”).

The creative rationale: [Y Combinator's first-party demo-day guide](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/guide-to-demo-day-pitches/) says to explain what the product does and for whom early, use only a few memorable points, and show evidence of progress. [Google's ABCD creative playbook](https://www.thinkwithgoogle.com/_qs/documents/6165/_Playbook_for_Creative_Advertising_-UK-_DEx45cw.pdf) recommends attracting attention at the start, showing the product in natural use, connecting through a story, and giving the viewer a clear next step. **Applying ad and investor-pitch guidance to this hackathon is my inference**, not a tested claim about this particular judging panel. Here, the memorable point is: *a camera watches the display during service, then shows the owner what remained and when a refill happened.*

## Shoot today: spend the two hours on irreplaceable evidence

Start the fixed tray-row capture immediately, then film the following with a phone. The priority is a continuous, time-stamped record of the same tray, not a polished cinematic sequence. If two minutes remain at the end, back up the raw images and clips before leaving. [YouTube's filming guide](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12340105?co=YOUTUBE._YTVideoType%3Dvideo&hl=en-GB) recommends a shot list, equipment checks, and backups; its [phone guide](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12948118?co=YOUTUBE._YTVideoType%3Dvideo&hl=en) recommends horizontal 16:9 footage, the rear camera, stable focus/exposure, and clear sound.

| Priority | Exact footage or record | How to capture it | What it proves |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Continuous raw Tray Watch JPEGs** plus `frames.csv` | Mount above the full row, aim down, check the crop, confirm the frame sequence advances. Keep original timestamps and filenames. | The prototype actually observed a real service over time. |
| 2 | **A fixed reference shot of the full display** | 8–10 seconds from the customer's side, with no identifiable customer in frame. Note which slot holds the named dish. | Where the product operates and how trays are displayed. |
| 3 | **Mounted camera and crop** | 5–8 seconds showing mount → camera → live/captured food-only view. Film the screen close enough to read the frame. | Physical prototype and privacy-conscious field setup. |
| 4 | **Same named tray at two or more real times** | Save original captures, write actual wall times, film a 5-second matching phone shot if possible. Keep angle and crop consistent. | The tray changed or remained full during the observed window. |
| 5 | **Real refill, if it occurs** | Capture a short phone clip from the food side without interrupting service; record the time and which tray. Preserve frames immediately before and after. | A genuine top-up, not a cut that suggests one. |
| 6 | **Owner's reaction, only if she agrees** | Ask on camera: “When would this information change how much you prepare or top up?” Record the answer verbatim. If she prefers not to appear, write down her words and attribute them as field notes with permission. | Target-user feedback and a concrete decision, including disagreement. |
| 7 | **End-of-observation shot** | Capture the same tray just before leaving; log time. If the device can stay safely through close, arrange retrieval of closing files. | The state at a *specific time*. It is only “at close” if actually captured at close. |
| 8 | **Evidence slate** | Photograph your handwritten log: date, stall, dish, slot, installation/start time, every observed refill or tray swap, exit time, and owner permission. | Lets you edit and analyze without inventing sequence or identity. |

The stall is documented as operating roughly 08:00–19:00 in the [PRD](09-traywatch-prd.md), but verify today's actual hours with the owner. If you leave before close and cannot retrieve later frames, your final shot is an *end-of-visit* observation. Label it that way.

**Privacy while shooting.** Ask the owner about filming her stall and obtain specific agreement before recording her face or voice. Aim the Tray Watch camera at food only; keep customers and staff other than consenting speakers out of promotional phone shots, and inspect the actual crop. [YouTube's filming guidance](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12340105?co=YOUTUBE._YTVideoType%3Dvideo&hl=en-GB) explicitly recommends obtaining location/bystander permissions and protecting other people's image, voice, and personal information. This is a practical production rule, not a legal conclusion about any particular filming situation.

## Timed storyboard for the main video (about 70 seconds)

| Time | Picture | Suggested narration or on-screen text |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–6 s | Two real images of the **same named tray** at different times; start on the strongest visible difference. | “What happens to this tray between serving and closing?” Show both actual times. If there is no visible change, start on the real refill or the still-full tray. |
| 6–15 s | Brief display/stall shot; owner's real words if permitted. | “The stall owner told us vegetables can be left over. She needs evidence to decide when to cook or top up.” Attribute the statement and avoid implying that every unsold item is thrown away. |
| 15–25 s | Camera mount, food-only crop, a saved JPEG appearing with timestamp. | “Tray Watch photographs the display every two minutes while service continues.” Say this only if the cadence was verified in today's file. |
| 25–42 s | Actual time-lapse or matched raw frames of the **same slot and dish**; pause on any observed refill. | Show a visible clock/time stamp and a simple label: “Observed [start]–[end] on 23 Sep.” Mark “refill observed at [time]” only if supported by raw frames or your field log. |
| 42–55 s | Side-by-side first and last usable frames, then a close-up of the one-page report or a plainly marked draft. | “At [time], [dish] looked [observed state]. This can inform [one specific possible next action].” If no numerical analysis was validated, use visual description, not a made-up percentage. |
| 55–65 s | Owner's reaction or a written quote with permission; otherwise your own field note and next validation step. | “We asked the owner whether that changes a decision. She said [exact response].” If unasked, say “Next: review this with the owner.” |
| 65–70 s | Camera and same tray, clean title card. | “Tray Watch: evidence from the display, while there is still time to act.” Label future benefits “potential,” not achieved waste reduction. |

The precise run time is an editorial choice, not a research finding. In the booth loop, remove the long spoken explanation, retain timestamps and captions, and make the first and last frame each state the product in a few words so the loop is intelligible from any entry point. The [Tray Watch plan](05-traywatch-plan.md) already proposes a forty-second real-frame timelapse, the camera beside it, and a no-network table presentation. The 60–75 second version adds enough context for the submission and a guided booth conversation.

## What the footage can honestly claim

| Evidence you actually obtain | Wording you may use | Wording to avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Same tray photographed for two hours | “We monitored one tray during a two-hour service window.” | “We monitored a full day/week.” |
| Real refill in the footage and log | “A refill occurred at approximately [time].” | “The refill caused waste” without close/disposal evidence. |
| Tray still contains food when you leave | “The tray was [visually/estimated] full at [time].” | “Leftover at close,” if filming ended earlier. |
| Actual final closing frame | “The tray still contained food at close.” | “That food was thrown away,” unless disposal was observed and recorded. |
| Owner gives feedback | “The owner said [exact words].” | “The owner adopted our recommendation,” unless she actually did. |
| One day of frames | “A one-day field test showed [specific finding].” | “The system reduced waste” or “weekly report validated” without further observation. |
| No refill, no clear trend, or capture failure | “This field test exposed [specific limitation]; here is the next change.” | Editing a simulated top-up into real footage. |

The code adds two specific truthfulness constraints. [`analyse.py`](analyse.py) maps dish names by **day and slot**; if the owner swaps trays during the observation, the current analysis can attribute multiple dishes to one slot. Keep a time-stamped swap log and do a manual visual comparison by dish until this is fixed. [`report.py`](report.py) currently labels its first image “full tray”; the first image may be from mid-afternoon, and a last captured frame may not be closing. For the video, use the actual capture times on screen, and inspect any generated page before showing it as a result. A short field test can demonstrate capture and produce a useful observation; it cannot yet establish that a recommendation reduced waste. This distinction also follows the organizer's request to separate the built/tested prototype, findings, actual impact, and future scaling in the [kickoff transcript](../hackathon%20documents/20260826%20SG%20Eco%20Loop%20Kickoff.transcript.vtt).

## Edit and delivery

1. **Keep the originals.** Copy raw JPEGs, CSV, phone clips, and the time log into dated folders before editing. Make the edited time-lapse from duplicates; do not destroy metadata on the originals. This supports the organizer's requested evidence of testing and findings in the [kickoff transcript](../hackathon%20documents/20260826%20SG%20Eco%20Loop%20Kickoff.transcript.vtt).
2. **Show elapsed time honestly.** Use exact time labels, and mark a cut or time-lapse as “time-lapse.” Never imply the phone clip and fixed-camera frame were simultaneous unless their timestamps support it. Cut away to the mounted device only after the viewer has seen the real tray. This ordering is my editorial inference from [YC's clarity guidance](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/guide-to-demo-day-pitches/) and [Google's advice to show products in natural use](https://www.thinkwithgoogle.com/_qs/documents/6165/_Playbook_for_Creative_Advertising_-UK-_DEx45cw.pdf).
3. **Keep words readable in a noisy booth.** Put the dish name, observation window, and one finding on screen. Add accurate captions for speech and keep voiceover intelligible. The [W3C media accessibility guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/media/av/) calls for captions for speech and non-speech audio and descriptions of important visuals; a short descriptive transcript is useful for the submitted version. Test the silent loop with the laptop muted.
4. **Edit for one story.** Omit broad statistics and technical architecture unless they explain a visible moment. [YouTube's editing guide](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/11221953) recommends planning shots for the edit and adjusting pace to the viewer. Here, allow enough time to compare the two tray frames and read the timestamps.
5. **Export and rehearse.** Produce a 16:9 MP4 for the main video and a muted, captioned loop for the table. Test both offline on the actual laptop; open the video at several random points and ask whether a stranger can tell what was built, where it was tested, what was observed, and what remains unproven. The offline laptop loop follows the project's [showcase plan](05-traywatch-plan.md).

## Decision after today's shoot

Watch the raw frames before writing the final script. If you catch an actual refill and a later still-full tray, center the edit on **“the late top-up we could see.”** If there is no refill but the tray remains full, center it on **“a visible leftover risk at [actual time].”** If the tray empties, center it on **“the tray sold through; the measurement can prevent an unnecessary reduction.”** If the camera fails or framing is poor, make the honest story **“the first field test found the mounting/crop problem and what we changed.”** Those are scenario-dependent scripts, not assertions that any result has occurred.
