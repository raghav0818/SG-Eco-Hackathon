## Research finding: how to show an MVP credibly

The deck should answer **what it does, for whom, what was built, what was learned, and what evidence would justify expansion**. Put the product and user near the start. Use an actual photograph or legible crop of the prototype when that makes the claim clearer. Give each projected slide one explicit takeaway; put methodology, source notes, and longer explanations in compact captions or a readable appendix of the submitted PDF. This adapts [Y Combinator's Demo Day guidance](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/guide-to-demo-day-pitches/) and its [slide design guide](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/how-to-design-a-better-pitch-deck/) to a hackathon where judges also read the PDF. YC's very sparse live-pitch format is **not** a reason to omit the organizer's required detail from the submitted version.

The event's Best Innovation rubric weights **problem understanding, originality, feasibility, potential impact, and pitch/clarity** equally, as the organizer says in the [kickoff transcript](hackathon%20documents/20260826%20SG%20Eco%20Loop%20Kickoff.transcript.vtt) at 00:11:59. Make evidence for each visible. The separate Biggest Impact prize and Waste Diary accept weighed diversion that need not be caused by the prototype. Therefore, never put general Waste Diary kilograms on a prototype-impact slide without identifying that distinction. [Source: kickoff deck, pages 5–6](hackathon%20documents/20260826%20SG%20Eco%20Loop%20Kickoff%20Slides.pdf); [source: organizer email](hackathon%20documents/email.txt).

### Evidence labels to use consistently

| Label on slide | Meaning | Acceptable example |
|---|---|---|
| **Field observation** | What was seen or recorded in the real target setting | Dated, captioned installation photo or contemporaneous observation |
| **Controlled demo** | A staged test of a defined part of the workflow | Home bowl or keypad scenario with known input and visible output |
| **Software simulation** | Output produced from fabricated inputs or replayed data | Synthetic forecast or generated report, visibly marked synthetic |
| **User feedback** | An actual person's attributable response, with role and date | Cookhouse or stall operator comment recorded with permission |
| **Proposed pilot / assumption** | Something to validate after the hackathon | Expected user response, adoption rate, or waste saving estimate |
| **Measured impact** | A weighed, attributed outcome with a comparison method | Comparable baseline and pilot leftover kilograms, with dates and sample size |

No field visit, photo, or controlled demonstration by itself establishes avoided waste. If the prototype has no defensible attributable kilogram result, state **“prototype-attributable waste diverted: not measured yet”** and show how a pilot will measure it. A real before/after comparison should log food type, mass, date, service context, and the operational decision made; a simple before/after result also needs discussion of other causes of change. The [US EPA's food-waste assessment resources](https://www.epa.gov/sustainable-management-food/resources-assessing-wasted-food) support measuring amount, type, and source of waste rather than treating a visual proxy as kilograms.

## Thirteen-slide submission skeleton

The title of each slide is a **claim**, not a section label. Replace bracketed prompts with verified material. One large visual can carry the main claim; a small evidence line can carry dates, setting, sample size, and caveats. Use speaker notes or an appendix for dense method detail.

| # | Claim to establish and content to show | Best visual / evidence | Organizer requirement covered |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. One-line proposition** | “[Prototype] helps [specific operator] make [specific waste-preventing decision] at [specific moment].” Show one real prototype image and label its current maturity. | Real photo of the device or user-facing screen, captioned. | Concept, clarity. |
| **2. Problem and why chosen** | Name the waste-generating decision, where in the workflow it occurs, and why this team chose it. | Simple current-workflow image or annotated site photo. | Problem; why chosen. |
| **3. Scale, users, and unknowns** | Identify the primary user, decision-maker and other affected people. Size the **reachable first setting** with an actual count or source-backed estimate. State what was learned about the problem and what remains unknown. A national figure may add context but is not the addressable market. | Small sourced calculation, dated discovery notes, or short attributed user quote; “unknown → next test” line. | Problem size, users/others, learning and remaining questions. |
| **4. Real-world concept** | Explain the intended deployment loop: trigger → input → output → operator action → waste-prevention hypothesis. Include comparison to the current workaround without claiming exclusivity. | Three-to-five-step operator journey diagram, marked “proposed concept.” | Concept and how it would work in real use; originality. |
| **5. Actual build** | Show only functions actually implemented and where the prototype stops short of the concept. | Labeled photo or legible crop of working interface/hardware; small “built / still proposed” line. | What was built; how it represents the concept; feasibility. |
| **6. Test: what, where, with whom** | State test question, site, date, participants/roles, task, what was observed, and limits. Distinguish real setting from staged or synthetic tests. | Test photo with date/site caption and short method box. | How tested, with whom, where; design/build/test photos. |
| **7. Findings and changes** | Show one or two observations from the test and the product decision they caused. Say what the test did not prove. | Observed input/output pair, user action, or “finding → revision” diagram. | Findings and insights. |
| **8. Impact and waste diverted** | Give exact prototype-attributable kilograms **only if measured**. Otherwise state “not measured yet”; explain the pilot's baseline, weighing categories and comparison. Keep separate Waste Diary diversion in a clearly labeled note if relevant. | Simple evidence ledger: measured / inferred / unknown; no unlabeled hypothetical savings chart. | Impact: waste diverted using prototype; potential impact. |
| **9. Reflection** | One sustainability or design lesson; biggest challenge and response; skills the team developed. Be specific about a design or test decision. | Build iteration photo or “initial assumption → evidence → revision” pair. | Reflections, biggest challenge/response, skills. |
| **10. Next version and pilot decision** | State the most important refinement or pivot, first target site, minimal deployment, trial question, constraints, and a **pass/fail decision rule**. | Annotated future workflow with clear “proposed” label. | What to do if continued; execution plan; feasibility. |
| **11. Six-month route** | Give one measurable milestone per month, owner, evidence produced, and go/no-go gate. Use the timetable template below. | Large six-row timeline. | Next-six-month timeline; execution. |
| **12. Stakeholder path** | For each person or organization, specify permission or behavior needed, what they gain, how to approach them, and when. Distinguish interested parties from confirmed partners. | Stakeholder/decision map with outreach sequence. | Stakeholders involved and how brought in. |
| **13. Itemized budget and ask** | Show quantities, quote or estimate sources, one-time versus monthly cost, and six-month total. State what help is needed and the evidence the pilot would return. | Readable budget table and one-sentence pilot ask. Add acknowledgements in footer. | Budget breakdown; scaling; acknowledgements. |

For a short booth explanation, extract slides **1, 2, 5, 7, and 10** and let the live prototype or a short real demo supply the motion. Keep slides 8 and 11–13 available for judges' questions about impact and scale. This is an editorial recommendation, not an event format requirement.

### Six-month timeline template (October 2026–March 2027)

| Month | Work and stakeholder gate | Evidence to leave with | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| **M1 · Oct** | Obtain site/operator permission; document existing process and consent constraints. | Written scope, baseline protocol, process map. | Is an in-setting pilot permitted and safe? |
| **M2 · Nov** | Collect baseline without asking staff to change decisions yet. | Dated counts, weighed leftovers where permitted, notes on service volume. | Is the baseline usable and repeatable? |
| **M3 · Dec** | Run one tightly scoped prototype pilot with a named operator. | Uptime/error log, operator actions, comparable waste records, feedback. | Does the product reliably reach a decision-maker? |
| **M4 · Jan** | Fix the largest failure from M3 and rerun at the same site. | Repeated measures, changed workflow, documented failure cases. | Is there an early improvement signal without unreasonable burden? |
| **M5 · Feb** | Try a second setting or shift **only if** the first gate passed. | Transferability notes, installation/training time, incremental cost. | Does the idea survive different conditions? |
| **M6 · Mar** | Evaluate cost, waste, usability and operating responsibility with site partners. | Pilot report; per-site economics; stop/refine/expand recommendation. | Is wider deployment justified? |

This is a **proposed** route, not a claim that either prototype has a partner committed. A failed gate changes the next month to diagnosis or a smaller pilot; do not silently report expansion as guaranteed.

### Stakeholder and budget templates

| Stakeholder | Needed contribution | Benefit or concern to address | Outreach and evidence of agreement |
|---|---|---|---|
| Frontline operator | Use or respond to the prototype at the decision point | Time burden, trust, actual usefulness | Show working task, observe use; record consent/feedback |
| Site owner/manager | Permit installation, access, and process changes | Safety, privacy, service quality, responsibility | Scoped pilot proposal; written approval before deployment |
| Food/waste measurement lead | Define and run weigh-in protocol | Data quality and staff effort | Agree on baseline, categories, scale, and logs |
| Technical maintainer | Install, debug, and support equipment | Reliability and support load | Named owner, support process and maintenance cost |

| Budget line (S$) | Quantity × verified unit price | One-time / monthly | Source and owner | Why needed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hardware and mounting | `[qty] × [quote]` | One-time | `[vendor/date]` | Deployment at pilot site |
| Connectivity, hosting, or software | `[qty] × [quote]` | Monthly | `[vendor/date]` | Core data path |
| Installation and training | `[hours] × [rate]` | One-time/site | `[assumption or quote]` | Actual operator setup |
| Measurement and audit | `[hours/scale/material]` | Per test | `[source]` | Verify waste outcome |
| Maintenance and replacement | `[qty] × [quote]` | Monthly/site | `[source]` | Keep pilot running |
| Contingency | `[stated % of quoted subtotal]` | One-time | `[assumption]` | Known installation uncertainty |
| **Total / funding request** | **Sum shown lines** | Split capital and six-month running cost | State what is already owned | Make requested support reviewable |

The hackathon email mentions an **up-to-S$200 prototyping budget per prototype**. That is a programme reimbursement limit, **not** a justified six-month deployment budget. Keep prior spend and future pilot ask separate. [Source: organizer email](hackathon%20documents/email.txt).

## Slide craft and submission checks

- **Lead with the operator's decision and the product.** Do not make judges wait through several context slides to learn what the prototype does. [YC presentation guide](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/guide-to-demo-day-pitches/).
- **Make the live subset glanceable.** Use one point per slide, large legible text, sufficient contrast, and crop screenshots to the one feature being discussed. Detailed test tables can live in the PDF appendix. [YC slide guide](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/how-to-design-a-better-pitch-deck/); [W3C contrast guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG21/Understanding/contrast-minimum).
- **Caption real evidence.** Caption photos with date, place, what is happening, and the claim they support. Mark staged footage, renderings, and generated illustrations so they cannot be mistaken for field deployment. The organizer explicitly asks for design/build/test photos, captions, acknowledgements, and restraint with obvious AI output. [Organizer brief, page 1](hackathon%20documents/SG%20Eco%20Loop%20Hackathon%20Framework%20Reference.pdf).
- **Show the human interaction.** Add an actual user/operator response when one exists; AI-generated scenes do not substitute for one. If no interaction happened, say so and make it the first pilot gate. [Organizer brief, page 1](hackathon%20documents/SG%20Eco%20Loop%20Hackathon%20Framework%20Reference.pdf).
- **Export and inspect the final PDF at projected size.** Verify font size, image clarity, links, captions, slide order, and the 1 October deadline. Rehearse a short explanation and a separate live-demo fallback. [YC presentation guide](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/guide-to-demo-day-pitches/); [organizer brief, page 1](hackathon%20documents/SG%20Eco%20Loop%20Hackathon%20Framework%20Reference.pdf).
