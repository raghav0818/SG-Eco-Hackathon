# Hawker centre food waste — 5W1H, Singapore context

> **Purpose.** Desk research for the second prototype (hawker centres). The participant has no first-hand pain point here — *"I rarely eat out, and when I do there are no pain points for me when it comes to food wastage."* That statement is itself a finding; see §8.
>
> **Nothing here is `[testimony]`.** All `[cited]` or flagged. This is Discover input, not a problem definition. The HMW comes after fieldwork, not after this file.

---

## 1. WHAT is wasted

### National scale

| Figure | Value | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Food waste **generated**, 2025 | **~790,000 tonnes** | only **18%** recycled (~140,000 t) |
| Food waste **disposed of**, 2025 | **649,000 tonnes** | up from 646,000 t in 2024 |
| Share of Singapore's total waste | **~11%** | |
| Food services share of all food waste (2022) | **28%** | restaurants, hotels, caterers — hawker centres sit inside this |
| Commercial sector cost of food waste | **~S$342 million/year** | |
| Wasted food as share of sales, average food service | **5.6%** | |

**Note the two different numbers.** "Generated" and "disposed" are different measures and should not be used interchangeably in a pitch. Quote one, name it, and move on — a judge who spots you mixing them will discount everything else.

### Site-level scale — the numbers that actually land

| Site | Food waste |
|---|---|
| **Tiong Bahru Market** | **~3 tonnes per day** |
| **Ang Mo Kio Blk 628 Market** | **~2 tonnes per day** |

And the composition detail that matters most, from NEA's pre-trial audits: **most of it comes from market slab stalls and from table-cleaning operations.** Two streams, two completely different causes — one is stallholder-side, the other is diner-side.

### What the waste physically is

- **Avoidable:** leftovers, unsold cooked food, expired and stale stock
- **Unavoidable:** bones, shells, peel, trimmings
- **Carbohydrates dominate the avoidable half.** NEA: rice and noodles are the most commonly wasted items by diners. A Yale-NUS team that went through the bins reached the same conclusion independently.

## 2. WHO wastes

Four actors. **They are not variations of one user — they have different problems, different incentives, and different powers.** Picking between them is the whole Define decision.

| Actor | Their waste | Do they feel it? |
|---|---|---|
| **The stallholder** | Prep trimmings; over-prepared food unsold at close; held food past its window | **Yes — financially.** Thin margins, no storage, no volume discounts. Waste is money |
| **The diner** | Leftovers on the plate — mostly rice and noodles | **Mostly no.** Paid already, walked away, someone else clears it |
| **The table cleaner** | Handles every scrap of diner waste; one of the two largest streams | **Yes — physically.** And they are the only person who sees the aggregate |
| **The wet-market slab stall** | Unsold produce, fish, meat trimmings | Yes — but this is a market problem, not a cooked-food problem |

**One observation worth keeping:** the Yale-NUS team began by *interviewing cleaning staff and examining bins.* The cleaners were the fastest route to knowing what was actually being wasted. That is a repeatable method and it is available to you.

---

## 3. WHERE it happens

**Physical chain:** supplier → morning prep → cooking → **holding/display** → serving at the counter → the table → tray return → bin → on-site digester *or* waste-to-energy.

**Scale of the estate:**

- **123 markets and hawker centres** managed by NEA
- **~13,430 NEA-licensed hawker stalls** (2023)
- **On-site food waste digesters installed at ~10+ hawker centres**, leased by NEA from **Eco Wiz (SG)** and **VRM Operations (Singapore)**; stallholders and cleaners segregate, the machine converts to non-potable water or compost. One centre reported **~S$40,000/year saved** in disposal charges

**Two waste points that behave completely differently:**

- **The counter** — where the portion is decided. At a *cai png* stall the customer participates; at a chicken rice or laksa stall the bowl is fixed
- **The table** — where the decision is discovered to have been wrong, and where the cleaner inherits it

---

## 4. WHEN it happens

| When | What |
|---|---|
| **Morning prep** | Trimmings and unavoidable waste |
| **Through service** | Held food in *cai png* trays ages; presentation pressure keeps trays looking full |
| **Lunch peak** | Highest throughput, least time for any interaction at the counter |
| **End of service / end of day** | **Unsold cooked food.** The irreversible loss — it cannot be sold tomorrow |
| **After each diner leaves** | Table clearing — continuous, invisible, aggregated by the cleaner |

**The end-of-day loss is the hawker analogue of the cookhouse's unclaimed plate:** cooked, unsold, unrecoverable, and decided hours earlier.

---

## 5. WHY it happens

### Stallholder side

- **Thin margins, high overheads, rising rents.** No storage space, so no volume discounts — many pay retail rather than wholesale
- **Running out costs more than over-preparing.** A sold-out stall loses the sale *and* the regular who walked away. This is the **same asymmetry as the cookhouse**: being short is visible and punished, being over is silent
- **Display pressure.** A *cai png* tray that looks depleted deters customers. Abundance is a sales tool
- **Menu breadth multiplies risk.** Every additional dish is another prep batch that may not sell

### Diner side

- **Portions are too big.** **54%** of NEA survey respondents asked for smaller portion sizes in eateries and supermarkets
- **They don't like some ingredients** — cited directly in the same survey
- **Rice is the specific failure.** Most people do finish their rice; **the ~20% who don't, waste a lot of it**
- **Asking for less saves nothing.** *Cai png* is priced per dish item — rice is effectively free, so "less rice" carries a real cost (less food) and zero benefit (same price). **The price signal points the wrong way**
- **Nobody asks.** Bubble tea stalls ask about sugar level as standard practice. No one asks about rice

### The behavioural baseline (NEA, 2019)

- **64%** say they leave no leftovers when eating out — up 5% from 2015
- **56%** say they take away unfinished food — up 12%

Treat these as **self-reported and therefore optimistic.** The bins say otherwise. But they do establish that the *norm* is already pro-finishing, which matters: an awareness campaign here is pushing on a door that is mostly open. The residual waste sits with the minority who don't finish, and they waste a lot each.

---

## 6. HOW food becomes waste — the five mechanisms

1. **Over-preparation against uncertain demand** *(stall, end of day)* — irreversible, and driven by the short-is-loud asymmetry
2. **Held food ageing in the display** *(cai png trays)* — quality decays through service; presentation pressure prevents running trays down
3. **Fixed default portion, especially rice** *(counter)* — the default is set for the average appetite and nobody is asked
4. **Ingredient rejection** *(table)* — they didn't want that part of it; no portion tool fixes this
5. **Unavoidable prep waste** *(kitchen)* — bones, shells, peel. Baseline, not a target

## 7. The comparison you need before writing any HMW

**Your cookhouse thesis does not transfer, and assuming it does is the fastest way to lose this.**

| | SAF cookhouse | Hawker centre |
|---|---|---|
| Who sets the portion | The kitchen, hours before you arrive | **Largely you, at the counter** *(cai png)* |
| Can you ask for less? | **No mechanism exists** | **Yes — free, one word** |
| Who pays | Nobody at the point of eating | **The diner, per meal, at the moment of choosing** |
| Dollar voting | Absent | **Present and immediate** |
| Does the cook learn demand? | **No** | **Yes — daily sales, felt in the till** |
| Is there a feedback wire? | **Missing entirely** | **Present for sales. Absent for waste** |

**The cookhouse problem was "the feedback loop is missing." The hawker problem is not that.** The stallholder knows exactly what sold. What nobody knows is **what was thrown** — and the two are different numbers. A stall that cooked 100 portions and sold 80 knows about the 80.

**Three real gaps that survive this comparison:**

1. **The stallholder has sales data and no waste data.** Nobody weighs what goes in the bin, so the cost of over-preparing is never priced against the cost of running out
2. **The ask doesn't exist.** Bubble tea normalised "how sweet?" Nobody normalised "how much rice?" — and the price structure actively discourages taking less
3. **The cleaner sees everything and tells no one.** Same shape as the cookhouse service worker: the only witness, with no channel

## 8. Your own reaction is a finding

*"There are no pain points for me when it comes to food wastage."*

**That is almost certainly true of most diners, and it is the most useful sentence in this brief.** The diner pays, eats, leaves, and never encounters the consequence. They feel no friction because there isn't any — the system is designed to remove it, and the cleaner absorbs it.

Two consequences:

- **A diner-facing intervention is fighting an absence of pain, not a presence of friction.** That is the hardest kind of behaviour change and it is why the awareness literature performs so badly
- **The actors who actually feel pain are the stallholder (financially) and the cleaner (physically).** Neither is who you'd have picked by default

Do not resolve this from the desk. But go into fieldwork expecting the diner to be the *weakest* of the three personas, not the obvious one.

## 9. Already done in Singapore — do not re-propose

| Intervention | Who | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **Rice-level menu cards at mixed-rice stalls** | Yale-NUS "Better Policy Lab" team (Bryan Timothy, Lindy Quek, Ryan Siew, Wah Tzy Hyi), 2021 | **Trialled across four NUS canteens.** Bubble-tea-inspired. **This is the obvious idea and it is taken** — cite it, don't rebuild it |
| **On-site food waste digesters** | NEA + Eco Wiz, VRM Operations | Deployed at 10+ hawker centres. **Treatment, not prevention** — sits below reduction in the hierarchy |
| **Food waste conveyance system** | NEA, Our Tampines Hub | Tendered |
| **Mandatory tray return** | NEA, since 2021 | Nationwide |
| **Project Belanja!** | Food from the Heart + hawkers | App-driven; participating stalls at Mountbatten, Tampines, Kolam Ayer, Toa Payoh. **Redistribution, at small scale** |
| **Love Your Food** | NEA | National awareness campaign |
| **Lumitics** | SG startup | AI waste tracking — **hotels, not hawker centres** |
| **Treatsure** | SG app | Surplus buffet food |

**The reduction-vs-treatment point is worth making explicitly.** Singapore's hawker-centre response so far is overwhelmingly *treatment* — digesters, conveyance, co-digestion. Prevention sits above all of it in the waste hierarchy, and that framing is a potential-impact argument you can make honestly.

## 10. What to find out in the field — you cannot write an HMW without these

**Everything above is desk research. None of it is a problem definition.** Six questions, all answerable in a single afternoon at one hawker centre:

| # | Question | Who to ask | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|---|
| **H1** | How much food do you throw at close, and does it vary? | Stallholder | Sizes the irreversible loss |
| **H2** | How do you decide how much to prepare? Has anyone ever told you how much you threw? | Stallholder | Tests gap #1 — sales data without waste data |
| **H3** | What do you see most of in the trays? | **Cleaner** | The Yale-NUS team's own starting move, and the fastest route to truth |
| **H4** | Do customers ask for less rice? Would you charge the same? | *Cai png* stallholder | Tests gap #2 — whether the price signal really points the wrong way |
| **H5** | When you don't finish, is it the amount or the ingredients? | Diner | Splits mechanism 3 from mechanism 4 — no device can do this |
| **H6** | Does this centre have a digester? Who segregates? | Centre management / cleaner | Tells you what infrastructure already exists to build on |

**H3 is the highest-value hour available.** The cleaners handle the entire diner-side stream, are present and approachable, and are the only people in the building who see the aggregate. And unlike the cookhouse, **you can photograph all of it.**
